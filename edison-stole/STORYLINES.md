# Edison: three storylines

Vertical 1080×1920, 30 fps, about 45–55 s, for TikTok. Nothing is built until one is picked.

## The four cases (same in every storyline)

| # | What people think | What happened | Honest verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Edison invented the light bulb | Joseph Swan showed a working bulb in England in early 1879, months before Edison's. Swan had the British patent, so Edison had to merge with him: "Ediswan", 1883 | Not the first. He made it last longer and sold it |
| 2 | Edison invented the movie camera | His employee W.K.L. Dickson did most of the work on the Kinetograph (1889–1892). Edison's name went on the patent | Took the credit |
| 3 | (few people know this one) | Georges Méliès made "A Trip to the Moon" (1902). Edison's company copied prints and showed them across the US. Méliès got nothing from it | Stolen, plainly |
| 4 | Edison and Tesla were rivals | Tesla said Edison promised him $50,000 to fix his dynamos, then refused: "you don't understand our American humor". Tesla quit (1885) | Never paid. Source: Tesla's own account |

Wording rule: "stole" only for case 3. Cases 1, 2 and 4 say what happened ("wasn't first", "took the credit", "never paid"), so nobody can debunk the video in the comments.

## A. The People vs. Thomas Edison (recommended)

- Device and form: a trial with four counts; the gavel is the cut.
- What the viewer sees: a cartoon Edison in the dock, smug, holding a bulb. Each count brings a witness to the stand (Swan, Dickson, Méliès, Tesla) with one exhibit, and a verdict stamp slams down. He gets less smug with every count.
- First two seconds: Edison's face fills the frame behind the line "HE DIDN'T INVENT IT"; a gavel hits and the title "The People vs. Thomas Edison" stamps on.
- Beats: hook → count 1 bulb → count 2 camera → count 3 moon film → count 4 Tesla's $50,000 → final verdict "4 counts" → end card.
- Look: deep navy courtroom, flat 2D characters with thick outlines, gold only for the verdict stamps and the end card.
- Sound: low tense music bed, a gavel on every count, a stamp thud on every verdict, narration on top.

## B. The museum that lied

- Device and form: a myth turned over; one continuous camera glide down a museum hall.
- What the viewer sees: four glass cases, each with a brass plaque "Invented by Thomas Edison". At every case a hand strikes the name through and writes the real one, and the real inventor's portrait slides in front of Edison's.
- First two seconds: a brass plaque close up, "THE LIGHT BULB. Invented by Thomas Edison", and a red line already crossing the name out.
- Beats: plaque hook → bulb case → camera case → moon film case → Tesla's unpaid cheque → the hall with four corrected plaques → end card.
- Look: dark navy gallery, spot-lit cases, brass plaques, chalk-red correction marks.
- Sound: quiet museum-heist music, a marker scratch on each strike-through, a soft click as each case lights.

## C. Edison's desk (the bold one)

- Device and form: a character does the work; one frame that rebuilds, shot from above.
- What the viewer sees: Edison's desk from the top. Other people's inventions slide in with their owner's name tag; his hand peels the tag off, sticks on "T. A. EDISON" and drags it to his pile. The pile grows, then the tags all peel back at once.
- First two seconds: a hand peeling a name tag reading "J. SWAN" off a light bulb, with "NOT HIS" in big type.
- Beats: tag peel hook → bulb → camera → film reel → Tesla's IOU torn up → every tag flips back to the real name → end card.
- Look: top-down wooden desk, paper tags, ink stamps, gold for the real names.
- Sound: ragtime-style piano bed, a sticker peel and a paper slap on every swap.
